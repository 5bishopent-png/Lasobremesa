The Art of Staying

“A remarkable dinner has two endings. The final course—and the moment no one wants to leave the table.”

In Mexico, that unhurried time has a name: sobremesa. La Sobre Mesa exists to create the conditions in which it happens.
Our Culinary Language

Fire. Corn. Chile.
Sea. Cacao. Agave.

These are not motifs. They are working ingredients—the vocabulary of a kitchen shaped by years lived, studied and cooked in Mexico. We understand before we interpret, and we let technique serve pleasure rather than ego.

Meet the Chef

Chef Adam Bishop

More than twelve years living in Mexico—including Puerto Vallarta, Jalisco and Mexico City—shaped a culinary point of view grounded in place, regional ingredients and the generosity of the shared table. At La Sobre Mesa, that lived experience meets formal Franco-Mexican culinary training, disciplined technique and the confidence to know what to change—and what not to.

Place · Memory · Hospitality

Mexico is not our theme.
It is part of our culinary language.

Puerto Vallarta, Mexico City and Jalisco’s agave country inform more than what we cook. They shaped how we understand hospitality: the full table, the instinct to keep feeding, the absence of hurry and the belief that eating is simultaneously craft, memory and social language.
